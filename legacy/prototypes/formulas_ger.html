📊 Cap-Table & Valuation Arithmetic

Pre-money, post-money & %stake
post-money = investment / %stake pre-money = post-money − investment %stake = investment / (pre-money + investment)
MeansPost-money INCLUDES the new cash; pre-money EXCLUDES it. Stake is share of the post-money pie.
EgPre ₹60cr, raise ₹15cr → %stake = 15/(60+15) = 15/75 = 20%. Invest ₹25cr for 25% → post = 25/0.25 = ₹100cr.
TrapDivide by POST-money, not pre (15/60 = 25% is the planted error). Don't swap pre/post.
Dilution
new % = old % × (1 − dilution fraction) absolute share count is UNCHANGED in a primary round
MeansIssuing new shares cuts your percentage, not your share count. Can still raise your wealth if value rises.
EgFounder 800 of 1,000; issue 250 new → 800/1,250 = 64% (still owns 800 shares).
TrapDilution does NOT reduce absolute shares, and is not always value-destroying.
% equity at exit & exit multiple
required realisation = investment × target multiple %equity at exit = required realisation / exit valuation exit multiple = (%stake × exit valuation) / investment annual return = multiple^(1/n) − 1
MeansWork from the investor's realisation (their slice), not the whole company.
EgInvest ₹20cr for 25%, exit ₹200cr → realisation 0.25×200 = ₹50cr → multiple 50/20 = 2.5x.
TrapMultiple uses the investor's realisation, not exit valuation (200/20 = 10x is wrong). A multiple ≠ an annual rate.
Required stake from a target annual return
required %stake = investment × (1 + r)^n / exit equity value
MeansGrow the investment to its future value at the target return, then divide by exit equity.
Eg₹10cr at 30% for 5 yrs → FV = 10×1.3⁵ ≈ ₹37.13cr; exit equity ₹186cr → stake ≈ 20%.
TrapDon't skip the (1+r)ⁿ compounding. A 16× multiple over 4 yrs = 100% p.a., not 16%.
Adjusting entry equity for later-round dilution
entry % = exit % / (1 − next-round dilution) the final cap table must sum to 100%
MeansAn early investor needs a bigger entry stake because later rounds dilute them.
EgNeed 34.7% at exit, round 2 takes 35% → entry = 34.7/(1−0.35) = 53.3%.
TrapGross UP for dilution (entry > exit %); always check the cap table sums to 100%.

📐 Valuation Ratios

P/E and EPS
EPS = PAT / shares ; P/E = price / EPS
MeansPrice per ₹1 of earnings. Within an industry, firms share similar P/E (an industry characteristic).
TrapP/E is meaningless for loss-making firms (no PAT) — use EV/EBITDA instead.
P/B and book value per share
net worth = share capital + reserves & surplus BVPS = net worth / shares ; P/B = price / BVPS
MeansPrice relative to book equity. P/B < 1 may signal undervaluation.
EgPrice ₹200; 10,000 sh × ₹100 + ₹5,00,000 reserves = ₹15,00,000 → BVPS 150 → P/B = 200/150 = 1.33.
TrapUse book value (incl. reserves), not face value (200/100 = 2 is the trap).
Enterprise value (EV) & EV/EBITDA
EV = equity market value + debt − cash equity value = EV − debt (− preference)
MeansValue of the whole firm. EV/EBITDA is independent of capital structure → best for loss-makers.
EgEquity ₹800cr + debt ₹300cr − cash ₹100cr = EV ₹1,000cr.
TrapAdd debt, subtract cash (adding cash → ₹1,200cr is wrong). EV ≠ equity value.
Comparables (multiples) method
justified price = target metric (EPS / EBITDA / sales) × industry-average multiple
MeansValue relative to similar listed firms or recent comparable transactions.
TrapP/E fails for loss-makers or firms with very different growth — switch to EV/EBITDA or EV/sales.
Unit economics & CLV
contribution margin = (price − variable cost) / price CLV = (revenue per period × contribution-margin %) / churn compare via CLV / CAC (healthy ≫ 1)
MeansPer-customer value vs the cost to acquire/serve. Positive contribution = strong unit economics.
EgEarn ₹500/customer, spend ₹600 → negative unit economics.
TrapUnit economics ≠ revenue scale; CLV falls as churn rises (churn is the denominator).

💸 Cash Planning

Cash budget (closing balance)
closing cash = opening cash + cash RECEIPTS − cash PAYMENTS (cash basis, NOT accrual P&L)
MeansTracks money actually in/out each period; its purpose is to estimate the funding requirement.
EgRevenue ₹5L (only ₹2L in cash), pay ₹3L, open ₹1.5L → 2 + 1.5 − 3 = +₹0.5L surplus.
TrapUse cash received (₹2L), not accrual revenue (₹5L); and don't drop the opening balance.
Net cash burn & runway
net cash burn = cash payments − cash receipts runway = cash balance / monthly burn
MeansHow fast cash is consumed and how long until it runs out.
EgReceipts ₹2L, payments ₹5L → net burn = ₹3L.
TrapBurn nets off receipts (not total spend); high burn ≠ profitability.
Total funding requirement
total funding = peak cumulative operational cash deficit + CapEx (infra + technology)
MeansHold the worst cumulative cash hole as a buffer, plus one-off capital spend, so you never run dry.
EgOutflow ₹100L + tech ₹50L − inflow ₹80L = ₹70L deficit to fund.
TrapInclude CapEx and use the peak cumulative deficit (not a single month, not accrual profit).

🛡️ Investor Protection Maths

Liquidation preference (the investor picks the higher)
non-participating = max( multiple × investment , ownership% × proceeds ) participating = multiple × investment + ownership% × (proceeds − multiple × investment)
MeansPriority payout. Non-participating: take the preference OR convert. Participating: take it AND share the rest.
Eg₹10cr/20%, exit ₹100cr, 1× participating → 10 + 0.2×90 = ₹28cr. 2× non-participating, exit ₹200cr → max(40, 40) = ₹40cr.
TrapParticipating: don't forget the pro-rata add-on. Non-participating: take the max (convert when equity beats the preference).
Anti-dilution: full ratchet vs weighted average
full ratchet: early-investor shares = investment / new (lower) price ← max protection weighted average: conversion re-prices BETWEEN old & new broad-based (incl. options/ESOP) < narrow-based protection (more founder-friendly)
MeansProtects an early investor in a DOWN round (price below the prior round).
EgSeries A ₹2.5L at ₹1 → full ratchet to ₹0.50 → 5,00,000 shares; cap table 52.6% / 26.3% / 21.1% (sums to 100%).
TrapFull ratchet = most protection (resets to new price); broad-based weighted avg = least. Cap table must total 100%.

🧱 Share-Capital Hierarchy

Terms of share capital (descending)
authorised ≥ issued ≥ subscribed ≥ paid-up face value = nominal value/share (any multiple of ₹1); premium above it → securities-premium account
MeansAuthorised = ceiling in the MOA; paid-up = money actually called and paid.
Eg10% dividend on a ₹10 face value = ₹1/share (dividend % is on face value).
TrapAuthorised (a ceiling) ≠ paid-up (actual); face value ≠ market or book value.
Net worth & market cap
net worth = share capital + reserves & surplus market cap = market price × shares
MeansNet worth is book equity; market cap is the market's value of equity.
TrapNet worth includes reserves (not just share capital); BVPS uses net worth, not face value.

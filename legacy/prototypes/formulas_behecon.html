⏳ Intertemporal Discounting

Discount factor δ (one period)
U = u₀ + δ·u₁ , δ ∈ (0,1)
MeansHow much you weight tomorrow vs today. High δ = patient, low δ = impatient.
UseTwo-period choices; the marshmallow-test intuition.
EgDinner today (u=10) + next week (u=10), δ=0.9 → U = 10 + 0.9×10 = 19.
TrapDirection: higher δ = MORE patient. δ can't exceed 1.
Exponential (Samuelson) discounting — time-consistent Econ
U⁰ = u₀ + δu₁ + δ²u₂ + … = Σ_{t≥0} δᵗ·uₜ (β = 1)
MeansConstant discount ratio between any two periods ⇒ plans never reverse.
UseAny "as an Econ…" question; benchmark against present-biased agents.
EgPV of u₂ is δ²·u₂ (discounting compounds), not δ·u₂.
TrapExponential discounters are time-consistent — they do NOT switch plans.
Quasi-hyperbolic (β–δ) present-biased discounting
U⁰ = u₀ + β·Σ_{t≥1} δᵗ·uₜ , β < 1
Meansβ discounts the whole future block (not u₀): impatient now, patient later ⇒ inconsistent plans.
UseNaïf/sophisticate movie & egg-roll/gym problems (β=½ or ⅗, δ=1).
Egβ=½, δ=1: value today of (u₁=5, u₂=8) = 0 + ½(5+8) = 6.5.
Trapβ multiplies all future periods, never u₀, and not just t=1.
Present-action payoff: vices vs virtues (δ=1)
Vice now: U = B − βC (immediate benefit B, delayed cost C) Virtue now: U = −C + βB (immediate cost C, delayed benefit B) Either, planned for the future: U = β(B − C)
MeansPresent bias prepones vices, postpones virtues. Compare "do it now" vs "plan to do it later".
EgEgg roll B=3,C=4,β=½: now = 3−2 = +1 (eats); planned = ½(3−4) = −0.5 (won't) → reversal.
TrapFor a present action only the delayed side gets β; when both are future, β multiplies (B−C).
Naïf vs Sophisticate (same β, δ — only belief β̂ differs)
Naïf: believes future self patient (β̂ = 1) → re-optimises, plans fail Sophisticate: knows future self biased (β̂ = β) → backward induction, pre-commits
MeansBoth present-biased; differ in self-awareness, so they end on different choices.
EgMovies a=3,b=5,c=8,d=13 (β=½,δ=1): Econ skips a, naïf ends on c, sophisticate on b.
TrapSolve the sophisticate by backward induction, not forwards. β and δ are identical across the two.
Solving δ from observed indifference
value_now = δⁿ · value_future ⇒ δ = (value_now / value_future)^(1/n)
MeansBack out the per-period factor across an n-year gap.
EgIndifferent between $40 today and $80 in 2 yrs → 40 = δ²·80 → δ = √0.5 ≈ 0.71.
TrapDon't forget the exponent: 0.5 is δ² (one-year), not δ. 1.41 is 1/δ.

💹 Prospect Theory & Utility under Risk

Prospect-theory value function
v(x) = x^β for gains (x ≥ 0) v(x) = −λ·(−x)^β for losses (x < 0) β ≈ 0.88 (diminishing sensitivity), λ ≈ 2.25 (loss aversion)
MeansValue over changes from a reference point: concave in gains, convex & steeper in losses (S-shape).
UseBundling / hedonic-editing comparisons; any "value of a gain vs loss".
Trapλ multiplies losses only; β < 1 (not >1). Always anchor at the reference point.
Loss aversion (λ)
v(loss) = −λ · v(equal gain) , λ ≈ 2 ⇒ WTA > WTP (endowment effect)
MeansA loss hurts ≈ twice as much as an equal gain pleases.
EgCornell mugs: sellers ask ~$7 (WTA), buyers offer ~$3 (WTP).
TrapEndowment effect = reference points + loss aversion. NOT risk aversion or probability weighting.
Bundling / hedonic editing (apply v(x) curvature)
SEGREGATE gains (two gains > one combined, concave) INTEGRATE losses (one big loss < two losses, convex) INTEGRATE small loss into big gain (cancellation) SEGREGATE small gain from big loss (silver lining)
MeansDecide whether to combine or split outcomes to maximise felt value.
EgPay bonuses in separate instalments (segregate gains); bundle penalties (integrate losses).
TrapIt's segregate-gains / integrate-losses — the reverse is the planted error.
Expected utility, risk aversion & certainty equivalent
EU = Σ pᵢ·u(xᵢ) risk averse ⇔ u concave ⇔ E[u(X)] < u(E[X]) (Jensen) Certainty equivalent CE: u(CE) = E[u(X)]
MeansChoose by average of utilities, not average payoff. Concave u ⇒ prefers the sure thing.
Use"Which would a risk-averse person choose / what's the CE".
TrapConcave (not convex) ⇒ risk aversion; and E[u(X)] < u(E[X]), not >.
Risk premium
Risk premium = E[X] − CE (positive for a risk-averse person)
MeansMost a risk-averse person will pay to avoid the risk — what insurers extract.
EgEV ₹20,000, CE ₹16,000 → premium = 20,000 − 16,000 = ₹4,000.
TrapIt's EV − CE (not CE, not CE − EV).
St Petersburg paradox
EV = Σ (1/2ⁿ)·2ⁿ = 1 + 1 + … = ∞ EU = Σ (1/2ⁿ)·u(2ⁿ) → small finite when u concave (e.g. log)
MeansInfinite EV but people pay little → resolved by concave utility (diminishing marginal utility).
TrapPeople pay little because EU is finite, not because EV is small (EV is infinite).
Acquisition + transaction utility
Purchase utility = acquisition utility + transaction utility transaction utility = reference (fair) price − actual price
MeansYou value both the good (acquisition) and the "deal" vs a fair reference price (transaction).
EgBeer-on-the-beach: pay more for the same beer from a posh hotel (higher reference price).
TrapTransaction utility is about the deal, not the good's consumption value.

🎲 Probability & Judgement Rules

Conjunction rule
P(A ∩ B) ≤ P(A) and ≤ P(B)
MeansA joint event is never more probable than either component — violating this is the conjunction fallacy (Linda).
TrapMore detail = more conjuncts = lower probability, even though it feels more representative.
Probability weighting (decision weights ≠ probabilities)
small p → OVER-weighted (possibility effect) near-certain p → UNDER-weighted vs certainty (certainty effect) [S-shaped curve]
MeansPeople act on decision weights, not objective probabilities (Allais, lottery play).
TrapSmall probabilities are over-weighted (not under). Weights ≠ probabilities.

🎯 Strategic Reasoning

Level-k thinking (⅔-of-the-average beauty contest)
Level-0 ≈ 50 (random) Level-1 = ⅔×50 ≈ 33 Level-2 = ⅔×33 ≈ 22 Level-∞ → 0 (Nash)
MeansPeople do a finite number of reasoning steps; masses cluster at L1/L2.
EgFT readers clustered ~22 and ~33; winning guess ≈ 13. Range 20–100 → Nash = 20 (boundary).
TrapLevel-0 ≈ 50 (random); 0 is the Nash/level-∞ value, not level-0.
Best response to a known mix (level-2 example)
Guess = ⅔ × E[opponents' guesses], E = Σ (share_k × guess_k)
MeansForm the expected average from the believed type-mix, then take ⅔ of it.
Eg30% L0 (≈50) + 70% L1 (≈33): E ≈ 0.3×50 + 0.7×33 ≈ 38 → guess ≈ ⅔×38 ≈ 25–28.
TrapWeight by the believed shares; don't just take ⅔ of 50.
Cognitive Hierarchy (Poisson-τ)
Each type-k best-responds to a Poisson(τ) distribution over ALL lower types 0..k−1 Higher τ → more higher-order thinkers → LOWER average guess (and ↑ profit/survival)
MeansGeneralises level-k with a distribution (τ≈2). Goldfarb-Yang: τ≈2.66 for ISP managers.
EgGroup averages 35 / 26 / 17 → τ = 1 / 2 / 4 (lower average = higher τ).
TrapLower guess ⇒ higher τ. CH ≠ level-k (level-k assumes others are exactly one level below).

⚖️ Discrimination

Becker taste-based discrimination
effective wage of out-group labour = w·(1 + d) d = psychic cost of discriminating
MeansA "distaste" adds a psychic cost d, so the discriminator acts as if out-group wages are higher → hires fewer.
UseDistinguish from statistical discrimination (an information problem, no d).
TrapTaste-based is fixed by contact/intermixing (and competition), not by more information. Statistical is the information one.

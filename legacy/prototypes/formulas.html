🟩 Macroeconomics

Most macro is one-step arithmetic or a directional chain. Memorise these 13 — then practice them in the ⚡ Formula Speedrun.
1 · GDP identity (expenditure)
Y = C + I + G + NX  |  NX = Exports − Imports
MeansTotal output = Consumption + Investment + Government + Net exports.
Use whenA shock hits — ask which letter it moves first, then trace to AD.
C=60, I=20, G=15, NX=5 → Y = ₹100.
2 · Savings = Investment identity
S_private + S_public + S_foreign = I
MeansFrom (Y−C−T)+(T−G)+(Im−Ex)=I. The three savings pools fund all investment.
Use whenLinking a current-account deficit (foreign savings) to domestic investment.
Private 800 + Public (−200) + Foreign 100 → I = ₹700.
3 · Nominal vs Real growth
Nominal growth − Real growth ≈ Inflation (GDP deflator)
MeansNominal = current prices (inflation-contaminated); Real = constant prices (quantity).
Use whenAny growth claim — strip out inflation to judge real output.
Nominal +12%, inflation 8% → real growth ≈ 4%.
4 · Money multiplier
Multiplier = 1 ÷ CRR  |  Deposits = Base × (1/CRR)
MeansFractional-reserve banking turns base 'seed' money into deposit 'fruit'.
Use whenRBI changes CRR or injects liquidity; lower CRR → bigger multiplier.
CRR 5%, base ₹100cr → 1/0.05 = 20 → ₹2,000cr deposits.
5 · Bond current yield
Yield = Annual coupon (₹) ÷ Current price (₹)
MeansCoupon is fixed; price moves → yield and price move inversely.
Use whenReading the 'true cost of money'; rising yields = tightening/risk.
₹1,000 bond, 8% coupon (₹80), price ₹800 → yield = 10%.
6 · Fisher identity (real rate)
Real rate ≈ Nominal rate − Inflation
MeansThe real rate is the true economic cost of borrowing/saving.
Use whenJudging if money is genuinely 'tight' or 'loose'.
Loan 7%, inflation 7% → real 0% = essentially free.
7 · Fiscal multiplier
Multiplier = 1 ÷ (1 − MPC)
MeansEach ₹1 of spending is re-spent down a chain at rate MPC.
Use whenSizing stimulus; higher MPC (poorer households) → bigger bang.
MPC 0.8 → 1/0.2 = 5 → ₹100cr spend ⇒ ₹500cr GDP.
8 · Output gap
Output gap = Actual GDP − Potential GDP
MeansNegative = slack/unemployment; Positive = overheating/inflation.
Use whenDiagnosing whether to ease or tighten (the RBI's core gauge).
Actual 180, Potential 200 → −20 (slack → stimulate).
9 · Real exchange rate (PPP)
e = E × (P_foreign ÷ P_domestic)  |  long-run e → 1
Meanse > 1 ⇒ rupee undervalued (India cheap) → appreciation pull.
Use whenLong-run currency direction (Big Mac Index logic).
e = 1.2 → a US Big Mac buys 1.2 Indian ones → rupee undervalued.
10 · Interest Rate Parity (UIP)
Expected ₹ depreciation ≈ i_domestic − i_foreign
MeansNo-arbitrage: higher local rates ⇒ currency expected to depreciate.
Use whenPredicting short-run currency moves from rate gaps / capital flight.
India 7%, US 3% → rupee expected to depreciate ~4%.
11 · Exchange-rate quote
E = ₹ per $1  |  rupee 'price' = 1/E  |  E↑ ⇒ ₹ depreciates
MeansE rising means more rupees per dollar → rupee weaker, dollar stronger.
Use whenTranslating 'rupee fell' into the number and trade effects.
E: 80 → 84 ⇒ rupee depreciated; exports cheaper, imports dearer.
12 · Balance of Payments
Current Account + Capital Account = 0
MeansA current-account deficit is financed by a capital-account surplus (inflows).
Use whenFDI (patient) vs FPI (hot money) sustainability of a CAD.
CAD of $30bn ⇒ $30bn of net foreign capital must flow in.
13 · Debt sustainability
Debt/GDP stable if growth g > interest rate r; spirals if r > g
MeansIf the economy outgrows its interest bill, debt ratio drifts down.
Use whenJudging whether a fiscal deficit is dangerous or benign.
g = 7%, r = 5% → sustainable; g = 4%, r = 8% → spiral risk.

🟧 New Product Development

NPD is framework-heavy and formula-light — only two real calculations, both 1-mark Sec A items. Know them cold; everything else is classify/define.
1 · ATAR demand model
Demand = Market × Awareness × Trial × Availability × Repeat
MeansAll factors are fractions (0–1); multiply straight through.
Use whenForecasting new-product demand & spotting the weakest link (which lever to fix).
2,000,000 × 0.5 × 0.4 × 0.8 × 0.5 = 160,000 units.
2 · QFD weighting (House of Quality)
Tech weight = Σ(importance × relationship[9/3/1])  |  relative % = weight ÷ grand total
MeansRelationship score = 9 strong / 3 moderate / 1 weak. Higher weight ⇒ prioritise that spec.
Use whenTranslating the Voice of the Customer into engineering priorities.
Importance 4 × 9 + importance 5 × 3 = 36 + 15 = weight 51.

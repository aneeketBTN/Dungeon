# SPMS objective-question feedback — recurring paper shapes

Date recorded: 2026-08-22
Source: owner report after receiving and observing the SPMS question pattern
Format observed: MCQ plus multiple-right-choice MCQ / P-type questions
Status: canonical first-party feedback record; useful for later revision authorship

This record preserves the objective-question patterns the owner observed in SPMS. It is separate
from the later BRGSA post-paper caselet feedback because the format and evidentiary scope differ.

## Observed question families

1. **Direct concept recall** — identify the definition, method, framework or named idea.
2. **Framework or method anatomy** — identify what constitutes a framework or method, including its
   components, steps or boundaries.
3. **Change and consequence** — given that X changes, identify the likely effect on Y.
4. **Relationship interpretation** — interpret two concepts moving together or in opposite
   directions, such as “X rises while Y falls.”
5. **Statement verification** — choose which statements are true, relevant or entailed by a concept.
6. **Objective or purpose** — identify what a concept, framework or method is meant to achieve.
7. **Close-concept distinction** — distinguish two methods or concepts in the same domain.
8. **Case recommendation** — use a short case and select the recommendation supported by a named
   framework.
9. **Best-fit selection** — choose the framework or method most appropriate for a stated scenario.
10. **Outcome interpretation** — identify what a method or framework's result means, including mixed
    signals or two plausible outcomes.

The owner's representative mixed-signal example was: promoter score decreases while satisfaction
increases; interpret what that means. The important demand is not a long open-ended analysis but the
correct course-grounded reading of the relationship.

## Authoring contract derived from the feedback

- Keep the stem direct and ask for one primary move.
- Supply the scenario facts needed to decide; do not manufacture ambiguity for revision difficulty.
- Test exact vocabulary, framework components, purpose and close distinctions explicitly.
- Use directional or mixed-signal data only when the course supports a defensible interpretation.
- For multiple-right questions, make every correct statement independently defensible and every
  distractor traceable to a specific misconception.
- Give immediate correction during revision without turning the item into a second multi-step task.

## Scope boundary

These shapes are observed evidence for SPMS objective questions. Dungeon may use them as a loose
revision lens for objective practice in another subject, but must label that transfer as an
assumption. They do not prove another subject's format, topic weights or exact difficulty.

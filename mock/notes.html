🟦 New Age Business Models

M1 · Foundations of Business-Model Innovation3 pillars · BMC · traditional models · strategy vs model
A business model is the “story of how an enterprise operates.” Can’t tell the story? You don’t have a business — just a charity with a pitch deck. Netflix didn’t just swap DVDs for streaming; it killed the $40 late-fee frustration and replaced it with recurring value.

The 3 pillars (know all three)

PillarWhat it answers
Value PropositionWhat pain am I killing better than anyone?
Value DeliveryHow do I fulfil it (infrastructure + network)?
Value AppropriationHow do I capture enough value to profit (revenue/pricing/cost)?

Business Model Canvas — 9 blocks

  • Customer Segments · Value Propositions · Channels · Customer Relationships · Revenue Streams · Key Resources · Key Activities · Key Partnerships · Cost Structure.

Traditional models

  • Manufacturer (Tata, HUL): owns production/quality, weighed by factories. Metric = economies of scale (volume ↑ → unit cost ↓). Risk = high CAPEX.
  • Retailer (DMart, Reliance Retail): owns the customer + data, thin margins.
  • Razor-blade (bait & hook): sell handle cheap → lock into consumables (Gillette, HP ink).
  • Leasing: access to high-CAPEX assets for a fee (AirCap planes, GE medical) — removes upfront cost.
  • Bundling: package items to move inventory + raise AOV (MS Office, Happy Meal).
  • Franchise — parse [OWNER]-[OPERATOR]: COFO (company owns, franchisee operates) · FOCO (franchisee owns/invests, company operates) · FOFO (franchisee owns & operates).
  • Licensing = rent your brand/IP for a royalty (Disney). Contract mfg = hire a factory to make YOUR product (Apple–Foxconn).
⚠️ Trap: Strategy = WHERE to compete; Business model = the SYSTEM that implements it. A brilliant strategy with a broken model is a fast track to bankruptcy. Evolve-or-die: HMT (killed by Titan), Moser Baer (DVD → cloud).
M2 · The Platform Revolution & Network Dynamicspipeline vs platform · 4 players · network effects
Nokia & BlackBerry perfected hardware (pipelines) while Apple & Android built platforms. Pipelines chase supply-side scale; platforms chase demand-side economies of scale.

Pipeline vs Platform

  • Pipeline (linear): value flows one way (Ford → dealer → consumer); the firm creates all value.
  • Platform (multi-sided): value is co-created — Airbnb owns no rooms; host + guest create value together.

The 4 players

  • Owner (IP + rules — Google for Android) · Provider (interface — Samsung/Xiaomi) · Producer (app developers) · Consumer (users).

Network effects — value grows non-linearly

  • Connections = n(n−1)/2 — 100 phones = 4,950 links. Convex growth → fast monopolies.
  • Direct / same-side: more users help the same group (WhatsApp).
  • Indirect / cross-side: one group attracts another (PlayStation devs → gamers).
  • Two-sided loop: Visa merchants ↔ cardholders, each pulls the other.

Metrics

  • Liquidity (gold standard — can a buyer find a seller?) · Interaction failure rate (“No Ubers available”) · NPS. Vanity = app downloads (engagement pays the bills, not installs).
⚠️ Asymptotic Marketplace Effect: cutting Uber wait 10→3 min is huge; 3→1 min the rider doesn’t care, yet you’ve doubled drivers so each earns less. Past the plateau, extra supply destroys value.
M3 · Digital Marketplaces & the B2B RevolutionC2C/B2C/B2B · chicken-egg · GMV/take rate · disintermediation
Marketplaces took the ancient “Streets of Rome” and bolted on global transparency — fragmented trade → global procurement hubs.

Typologies

  • C2C (eBay, OLX) · B2C (Amazon, Nykaa) · B2B (Alibaba, IndiaMART).

Solving chicken-and-egg

  • Follow the Rabbit: seed with a non-platform success — Amazon used its own inventory first.
  • Piggyback: steal users from where they are — PayPal on eBay ($10 credit to prime the pump).
  • Seed the Platform: create value yourself — Google’s $5M Android app prizes.
  • Micro-market: own a niche first — Facebook @ Harvard. Win one dorm before the world.

IndiaMART — unit-economics masterclass

  • “Two-way discovery” engine with negative working capital — subscriptions collected upfront → more cash than revenue. Udaan wins on logistics + credit for SMEs.

Metrics

  • GMV = total value of goods sold · Take rate = % the platform keeps (Zomato ~25%).
⚠️ Two killers: (1) High GMV with ~0% take rate is a suicide note, not a business. (2) Disintermediation — plumber + homeowner meet on Urban Company then transact privately. Defend with insurance, trust, seamless payment.
M4 · SaaS & the FinTech Frontiermulti-tenancy · PLG · India Stack · LTV/CAC · NDR
SaaS is the “Nirvana” model: it converts CAPEX → OPEX (buy servers → rent a subscription). Predictable, scalable, hugely profitable when done right.

Core

  • Multi-tenancy (one infra serves all) · Scalability (10,000th user ≈ free → operating leverage) · MRR (investors pay high multiples).

Product-Led Growth (PLG)

  • The product is the salesperson — Slack, Calendly deliver value before capturing it (a Calendly link markets the product for you). Also know horizontal SaaS (broad — Salesforce) vs vertical SaaS (one industry — Toast).

India Stack (democratised credit)

  • Aadhaar (identity) + UPI (payments) + Account Aggregator (data/credit — share financials to prove creditworthiness instantly). FinTech segments: Payments · Lending (source→underwrite→disburse→collect) · Neobanks (branchless) · WealthTech · InsurTech.

Metrics

  • LTV ≥ 3× CAC or stop. CLV = ARPU ÷ Churn
  • NDR (Net Dollar Retention): 120%+ = revenue grows 20% from existing customers even with zero new signups.
⚠️ Sales-Model Mismatch (kills 90% of SaaS): you can’t sell a $10/mo low-touch product with a $100k/yr high-touch salesperson. Align price with sales effort.
M5 · Aggregators, On-Demand & D2CZomato economics · AOV/repeat · Casper & Peloton
The “Impatience Economy” values convenience above all. Aggregators don’t own the asset — they own the customer relationship.

Zomato unit economics

  • Take rate ~25% (22–30%) from the restaurant + ad revenue; often pays the rider more than the customer’s delivery fee covers. High-volume, thin-margin, rides on operating leverage.

D2C — the anti-pipeline

  • Mamaearth (toxin-free), BoAt (youth audio), Licious (cold-chain). Bypass retailers to own the data. Metrics = AOV and Repeat Purchase Frequency.

Learn from failure

  • Casper: a mattress is a ~10-year purchase → repeat frequency ≈ 0 → CAC eats you alive (no adjacents).
  • Peloton: treated pandemic pulled-forward demand as permanent, over-built supply → ~$40bn wipeout.
⚠️ Burn-led growth is a gamble: if margins don’t work at small scale, they won’t magically appear at large scale.
M6 · Creator Economy & Blockchain (+ Web3, ONDC, AI)80/20 · magic ledger · platform risk
We democratised passion: ~80M Indian creators, but only ~150k professionals (~$200/mo). The rest are hobbyists.

Creator lifecycle & metrics

  • Creation → Discovery → Monetization (Patreon, Graphy) → Growth. 80/20 rule (top 20% earn 80%). Actionable = conversion to paid; vanity = follower count.

Blockchain = the “Magic Ledger”

  • Everyone holds a copy; a trade writes to all copies. Each page has a hash (puzzle solution) → change one letter and the chain breaks → immutability → trust without intermediaries.

Web3 / tech you must name

  • Web 1.0→2.0→3.0: read-only → read-write (platforms) → read-write-own (decentralised, tokens).
  • Blockchain = the tech; Bitcoin = one app (capped 21M). Smart contract = self-executing code. NFT = unique, non-fungible ownership token (vs fungible crypto).
  • ONDC = open network/protocol for commerce (UPI-for-retail) — unbundles buyer app / seller app / logistics to break platform lock-in.
  • AI hierarchy: AI ⊃ ML ⊃ Deep Learning ⊃ Generative AI/LLMs; Agentic AI plans & acts.
⚠️ Platform Risk is the silent killer: if your income depends on the YouTube/TikTok algorithm, you’re a sharecropper on someone else’s land. Own your audience.
Metric ↔ Model map (core trap): Retail → sales/sqft · Leasing → utilisation/default · Distributor → inventory turnover · Marketplace → GMV + take rate · SaaS → MRR/NDR/churn/CLV · D2C → AOV/margin/repeat · Hotel → RevPAR.

🟩 Principles of Macroeconomics

⚠️ Exam reflex for written sections (60 min, tight): Identify the shock → name what shifts (AD or AS) → state the consequence → recommend the policy tool and its limit. The “limit” earns the top band.
M1 · Foundations & the Macro Lensnominal vs real · variables · identities · sleep analogy
Your room is a set of “fingerprints” of technology, institutions and money. Macro = the aggregation of millions of micro decisions via markets.

Three threads of history

  • Technology (4 industrial revolutions: steam → electricity → silicon chip → AI) · Institutions (tribes → empire → free-market state) · Money (decentralised cattle/beads → centralised fiat → crypto flirts with decentralisation again).

Macro variables = price–quantity pairs

Price (how much)Quantity (how many)
InflationGDP
Interest rateMoney supply
Tax rateGovt budget
Exchange rateTrade balance (NX)
Wage rateUnemployment

Nominal vs Real (the apples-and-oranges fix)

  • Nominal GDP = current prices (contaminated by inflation). Real GDP = constant base-year prices (isolates the quantity story). Nominal − Real growth ≈ inflation (the GDP deflator).

Short vs long run — the sleep analogy

  • Long run = supply-driven smooth trend (your 8-hr sleep capacity). Short run = demand-driven choppiness (lazy Sunday vs stressed Monday).

Identities

  • Y = C + I + G + NX rearranged → S_private + S_public + S_foreign = I. Vocab: price stickiness (menu costs), frictions (sand in the gears).
M2 · Money, Banking & the Central Bankmoney multiplier · bonds · transmission · Fisher
Modern money is fiat — debt backed by trust, not gold. UPI is a wrapper on existing bank deposits: it moves money faster, it doesn’t create new money.

Money types & creation

  • Outside money = RBI cash (~5%). Inside money = bank deposits (~95%). Banks multiply seed → fruit via fractional reserves.
  • Money multiplier = 1 ÷ CRR → ₹100cr at 5% CRR creates ₹2,000cr.
  • M0 = base (reserves+currency) · M1 = currency+demand deposits (spend now) · M3 = M1 + time deposits/FDs.
  • CRR (cash with RBI) · SLR (govt securities held) · CAR (capital buffer). CPI = retail; WPI = wholesale; core strips food & fuel.

Bonds — price & yield move inversely

  • Yield = coupon ÷ price. ₹100 bond, 10% coupon, sells at ₹90 → yield 11.1%. Yield (not coupon) signals the true cost of money.

Dual mandate & transmission

  • Output gap = Actual − Potential. Target = CPI 4% ±2%; miss 3 quarters → explain to govt.
  • 5 channels: Interest rate · Credit · Asset price (wealth effect) · Balance sheet (collateral) · Expectations (forward guidance, hawkish vs dovish).
  • Real rate = Nominal − Inflation (Fisher). 7% loan, 7% inflation → 0% real = money “essentially free.”
⚠️ Limits: against a supply shock rate hikes “can’t grow tomatoes” → stagflation. At the Zero Lower Bound conventional tools die → QE. Crowding out: heavy govt borrowing ↑ rates → displaces private investment. FRBM Act caps deficits to keep fiscal space.
M3 · The Open Economy — Trade, Forex & the TrilemmaIRP · PPP · impossible trinity · BOP · 1991
Opening the economy “thickens the plot” — foreign forces now amplify or absorb every shock. Forex trades ~$7.5T/day; the USD is the “vehicle currency” (~88% of trades — a network effect).

The two anchors

  • IRP (asset market): India 7%, US 3% → rupee must depreciate ~4% (no-arbitrage).
  • PPP (product market): e = E × (P_foreign ÷ P_domestic) → converges to 1 long-run (Big Mac Index). e > 1 ⇒ rupee undervalued → buyers flock, demand rupees → appreciation pull.

The Impossible Trinity (pick 2 of 3)

  • Fixed exchange rate · Free capital mobility · Independent monetary policy. India = managed float (a deliberate middle ground, not a corner).

Balance of Payments

  • Current Account + Capital Account = 0. FDI = patient (factories, healthy); FPI = hot money (stocks, finicky — a nervous friend who wants it back in a shock).

1991 crisis (causal chain)

  • Deficits funded by hot money → Gulf War spiked oil & stopped NRI remittances → reserves ~2 weeks of imports → ~47 tonnes of gold airlifted to the Bank of England → birthed the managed float + liberalisation.
⚠️ Term traps: Appreciation/Depreciation = market-driven; Devaluation/Revaluation = policy-driven. FDI = patient capital (good); FPI = hot money (fragile) — financing a CAD with FPI is the 1991 setup.
M4 · Fiscal Policy & Growthmultiplier · deficit/debt · crowding out · FRBM · growth engine
The government's spending-and-taxing lever — it multiplies, it can crowd out, and over decades only one thing actually makes a nation richer: productivity.

The fiscal multiplier

  • Multiplier = 1 ÷ (1 − MPC). Each ₹1 spent is re-spent at rate MPC. Higher MPC (lower-income households) → bigger bang. e.g. MPC 0.8 → ×5, so ₹100cr ⇒ ₹500cr.

Deficits, debt & discipline

  • Debt sustainability: debt/GDP stabilises if g > r, spirals if r > g — that's the test for 'dangerous vs benign', not the deficit's size.
  • Crowding out: heavy govt borrowing ↑ interest rates → displaces private investment.
  • FRBM Act: legislated deficit limits → preserves 'fiscal space' to act countercyclically in downturns.
  • Countercyclical (Keynes): stimulate in slumps, consolidate in booms — never pro-cyclical.

Fiscal vs monetary

  • When monetary transmission is broken (credit channel jammed, or the Zero Lower Bound), fiscal policy injects demand directly — it doesn't need banks to lend. They're complements, chosen by situation.

Long-run growth

  • Long-run growth is supply-side: capital alone hits diminishing returns; sustained per-capita growth comes from technology / productivity (escaping the Malthusian trap). Demand policy only smooths the cycle — it can't raise the trend.
⚠️ Trap: don't answer a long-run growth question with demand stimulus — that's a supply/technology story. And un-anchored inflation expectations turn self-fulfilling (wage-price spiral).

🟧 New Product Development

⚠️ Core NPD trap: funnel-stage confusion & reversed dichotomies (modular↔integral, alpha↔beta, horizontal↔vertical, internal↔external integrity). Always fix the funnel stage before choosing the tool.
M1 · Why NPD, the 5 “News”, the PM & the Stage-GateReady-Fire-Aim · innovation mix · PM as GM · 10/100 rule
Nearly 50% of new launches fail. Most firms run the “Ready, Fire, Aim” trap — shooting products out with more hope than strategy. Standing still is the fastest way to fall behind.

Why innovate (or die)

  • Survive as a Differentiator (better) or Cost Leader (cheaper). Catch-up is brutal: rivals clone a flagship feature in 3–4 months (~120 days). Use an Innovation Mix to defend territory.

The 5 flavours of “new”

TypeExample
Breakthrough (new-to-world)2007 iPhone, laser printer
New product line (new to firm)P&G enters shampoo; Reliance into mutual funds
Line extensionLifebuoy → liquid handwash
Improvement / revisionAnnual iPhone cycle, Maruti updates
Reposition / cost-reduceAspirin → heart health; steel → plastic

The PM is a General Manager, not a specialist

Concept in action — the Multidisciplinary Navigator: the PM speaks three languages at once — Marketing (segment/position), Operations (supply chain/cost), Finance (feasibility/profit). Fail to bridge the silos and the product dies in the gap between “cool design” and “unviable cost.”

The Stage-Gate funnel

  • Concept Generation → Preliminary Assessment → Business Case → Development & Prototype → Pre-Commercialisation.
Every gate = two tests: the Narrative Test (who’s the customer, why pay?) AND the Numbers Test (can we make it at a price that profits?). Fail either → drop it.
⚠️ Product-centric vs user-centric — the Bottle Loft case: the firm screamed the magnet was “Strong Like a Bull” (a feature) while customers feared the 3M adhesive would fail and drop bottles on their heads. Obsessing over the engineering feat instead of the user’s real anxiety wasted the whole pitch.

Toolkit (TL;DR)

  • Innovation Mix: incremental “support” products fund the long-shot breakthroughs.
  • Front-End Loading — the 10/100 rule: a spec change costs ~nothing at idea stage, 10× in late development, 100× at launch. Solve design issues early.
  • Master the multidisciplinary: lead end-to-end as a GM, not a specialist.
M2 · Idea Generation & Opportunity Spottinglead users · frustrations · Buyer Utility Map · Morville honeycomb
Great products start as great problems. The richest ideas come from mining frustrations, not from another brainstorm of features.

Where ideas come from

  • Employees, customers, competitors — and especially product frustrations (a user’s anger is a market signal).
  • Lead users (three types): at the leading edge of the target market · in analogous/advanced markets · extreme users. They face needs months/years ahead and benefit from solving them, so they innovate first.
  • Lead-user study = systematically find & co-create with them. Limitation: their solutions can be niche/hard to generalise to the mass market.

Two opportunity-spotting maps

  • Buyer Utility Map (Blue Ocean): utility levers × the 6 experience stages — purchase, delivery, use, supplements, maintenance, disposal. Scan every cell for untapped utility rivals ignore.
  • Morville UX honeycomb (superior experience): useful · usable · desirable · findable · accessible · credible · valuable.
⚠️ Trap: don’t fixate on engineering features — anchor on the user’s holistic problem (see the Bottle Loft case in M1: the magnet was great; the customer feared the adhesive).
M3 · Concept Testing & EvaluationKano · QFD/House of Quality · ATAR · conjoint
Kill bad ideas cheap and on paper, before a single bit of steel is cut. This is the gate that protects the war chest.

Kano (feature prioritisation)

TypeAbsent → PresentExample
Must-beAnger → NeutralBrakes
One-dimensionalMore = betterBattery life
DelighterNeutral → DelightSurprise upgrade

QFD — House of Quality

  • Customer needs (WHATs + importance) as rows × technical specs (HOWs) as columns. QFD weight = Σ(importance × 9/3/1) → which specs to prioritise. The roof = correlation among technical specs (conflicts/synergies, e.g. lighter vs stronger).

Demand & preference tools

  • ATAR = Mkt × Awareness × Trial × Availability × Repeat (all fractions; multiply through).
  • Conjoint analysis: shows full-product bundles and decomposes overall preference into per-attribute part-worths → reveals the real trade-offs customers make.
  • Risk-Payoff matrix to rank concepts; Product protocol = the agreed spec “contract” before development; financial screen (incl. IRR — shared with GER).
⚠️ Trap: a concept test ≠ a market test. Stated-intent surveys suffer hypothetical bias (people overstate buying); behaviour-based methods (simulated test market) predict real trial far better.
M4 · Product Design & Prototypingmodular vs integral · 6 modularity types · DFM · prototyping
Architecture is destiny: it locks in your cost, variety and speed for the product’s whole life.

Modular vs Integral

  • Modular = standardised interfaces, mix-and-match, easy variety/upgrades (PC, bicycle). Integral = interdependent parts optimised as one whole — best performance, costly to change (iPhone internals, F1 car). Cases: Swatch (modular) vs Shimano (integral).

The six modularity types

  • component-swapping · component-sharing · bus (common backbone — PC motherboard) · sectional (standard interfaces, free config — Lego) · mix · cut-to-fit (adjust a dimension).

Prototyping & DFM

  • Design for Manufacturing (DFM) = design so it’s cheap/easy to build. Prototyping moves whiteboard → beta reality; choose in-house vs vendor. Case: Gillette Mach3 (heavy prototyping investment).
⚠️ Trap: the modular ↔ integral reversal. Modular = decoupled/swappable; integral = coupled/optimised. Don’t flip them.
M5 · Product-Use Testing, Teams & Servicesalpha/beta/gamma · org structures · integrity · PSS
Now find out if it survives contact with real users — and with real org politics.

Use-testing sequence

  • Alpha = internal team, controlled conditions → Beta = real external users in their natural environment → Gamma = final verification against specifications before launch.

NPD organisation structures (depth ↔ integration)

  • Functional (deep expertise, weak coordination) → Lightweight matrix (coordinator, little authority) → Heavyweight matrix (powerful PM, strong integration) → Autonomous / tiger team (dedicated, co-located, fastest — best for radical projects). Match: incremental → functional/light; radical → heavy/autonomous.

Product integrity (Clark & Fujimoto)

  • Internal = coherence between function and internal structure (engineering/org). External = fit between the product’s character and customer expectations/lifestyle. Independent — great products achieve both via a heavyweight PM.

Services & PSS — who owns the asset?

  • Product-oriented (you own it + services) · Use-oriented (provider owns, pay-per-use) · Result-oriented (buy the outcome — Rolls-Royce “Power by the Hour”).
⚠️ Traps: alpha ↔ beta (internal vs external) and internal ↔ external integrity are the two reversed dichotomies examiners love here.
M6 · Strategy, Differentiation, IP & Blue Oceanhorizontal/vertical · IP types · ERRC · BoP · design thinking
Fight in a red ocean and you bleed on price; create a blue ocean and you own uncontested space.

Differentiation & IP

  • Horizontal = taste-based, no agreed ranking (red vs blue car). Vertical = objectively better, everyone agrees (faster chip).
  • IP: patent (inventions, disclosed, time-limited) · trademark (brand identity) · copyright (creative works) · trade secret (undisclosed know-how — the Coke formula). Plus patent strategy & fighting design/digital piracy.

Blue Ocean toolkit

  • Strategy Canvas plots the value curve; ERRC grid — Eliminate & Reduce (cut cost) + Raise & Create (lift value) → breaks the value-cost trade-off, targets non-customers. Examples: IPL, iPhone, Southwest, CarryPro.

New markets & mindset

  • Emerging markets / Base-of-Pyramid: design for affordability & constraints. Design thinking: empathise → define → ideate → prototype → test (human-centred, iterative). Soft innovation / hyper-competition; case Luxottica.
⚠️ Trap: horizontal ↔ vertical reversal, and ERRC mixing up eliminate/reduce (cost side) with raise/create (value side).
M7 · Launch, Diffusion & Liabilityadopters · market testing · strict liability · recall
A brilliant product with a botched launch still dies. Land the beachhead, then expand.

Diffusion of innovation (adopter categories)

  • Innovators → Early Adopters → Early Majority (the bridge across the “chasm”) → Late Majority → Laggards. Mind the chasm between Early Adopters and Early Majority.

Launch planning

  • Strategic platform & target-market decisions; positioning (feature → function → benefit); brand vs trademark & naming; brand communities; pre-announcement; beachhead / lean launch; a launch-management system with innovation metrics; and finally product failure & deletion when it’s time to let go.

Market testing methods

  • Simulated → controlled → full test market (rising cost/realism) — validate the full marketing mix before a national launch.

Public policy & liability (flagged Sec A item)

  • Strict liability = liable for a harmful defect without proving negligence (defect + harm is enough). Contrast: negligence (failure of due care) & breach of warranty (broken product promise). Plus the public-policy issue life cycle, product recall, sustainable design & personal ethics in NPD.
⚠️ Trap: “legal basis for liability” is an explicit Section-A item type — when defect + injury appears with no mention of carelessness, the answer is strict liability.

# Dungeon — Art Direction

## Creative thesis

**The Door is the summit, not the wall.**

Dungeon should feel like a dangerous climb toward understanding. The homepage must communicate that promise before the player reads a word:

- Ari is small, vulnerable, and choosing to continue.
- The Door is distant enough to feel earned, but bright enough to feel irresistible.
- The route between them is occupied by things that may be enemies, memories, or misconceptions.
- Darkness conceals the full scale of the challenge; light reveals only enough to invite the next step.

The target is a **crisp, graphic, painterly 2D world** with selective texture and controlled animation. It should no longer read as generic pixel art, but it should preserve the dramatic economy, tiny hero scale, and atmosphere of the current threshold scene.

---

## What Dungeon already owns

These are identity anchors. Preserve them.

### 1. The monumental threshold

The existing Door is severe, architectural, and much larger than Ari. Its scale turns a menu action into a mythic decision.

### 2. Ari's saffron accent

Ari's scarf is the human signal in a cold environment. Saffron should remain the player's color: courage, agency, and earned progress.

### 3. Cold cyan threshold light

The cyan seam already acts as a visual metaphor for insight. It should stay, but become part of a larger light journey rather than the only bright line in a symmetrical image.

### 4. Ruin, roots, fog, and age

The world feels abandoned rather than merely dark. Stone, timber, roots, rubble, dust, and fog imply many attempts came before Ari.

### 5. A solemn emotional register

Dungeon is playful through its systems, not through visual comedy. The world should be strange and expressive without becoming cute or flippant.

---

## What to incorporate from Slay the Spire 2

Mega Crit describes its sequel's visual direction as more **playful, crisp, lively, cinematic, and colorful**, while retaining its unsettling identity. Its art director also emphasizes recurring motifs, concealed faces, strange creatures, bigger sets, full-screen art, animation, and VFX.

Dungeon should translate those principles rather than reproduce Slay the Spire's creatures, UI, or exact rendering.

### Crisp silhouette hierarchy

Replace uniform pixel detail with large readable shapes:

- one unmistakable Door silhouette;
- one unmistakable Ari silhouette;
- two or three unusual enemy silhouettes;
- broad environment masses;
- texture only where the eye is meant to linger.

At thumbnail size, the scene should still read as **person → ascent → Door → light**.

### Graphic painterly rendering

Use:

- clean outer contours;
- irregular hand-drawn interior edges;
- three to five value groups per object;
- hard-edged light accents;
- broad, visible brush texture in sky, fog, and stone;
- selective grain rather than a full-screen pixel grid.

Avoid smooth digital airbrushing and avoid rendering every surface equally.

### Designed weirdness

Threats should not be generic skeletons or horned demons. Their anatomy should embody learning failure while remaining mythic:

- **The Misread** — a tall, folded silhouette whose head points one way and body another;
- **The Cascade** — a creature assembled from stacked plates or ledgers, each weight shifting the next;
- **The Blank** — a smooth faceless shape with a single missing section where features should be;
- **The Clockworn** — an asymmetrical watcher whose limbs hang like stopped hands.

The metaphor should be felt before it is explained.

### Selective color as navigation

Color should carry meaning, not decorate every surface:

- near-black indigo and bruised violet: uncertainty;
- deep moss and muted teal: the old Dungeon;
- cyan: insight, rules, and the Door's active magic;
- saffron: Ari, agency, and player-earned progress;
- pale amber/ivory: possibility beyond the Door;
- small hostile coral accents: danger and incorrect paths.

### Full-screen cinematic moments

Treat the homepage, boss arrival, level completion, and failure as authored illustrations. Regular gameplay can remain more modular, but these transition moments should briefly fill the screen and make the run feel larger than the interface.

### Liveliness without visual noise

Movement should come from a few purposeful systems: breathing light, drifting particulate, cloth response, silhouettes shifting in fog, and layered parallax. The world should feel watchful, not busy.

Official references:

- [Mega Crit on Slay the Spire 2's art direction](https://megacrit.com/news/2024-10-02-neowsletter-issue-3/)
- [Official Slay the Spire 2 Steam page and screenshots](https://store.steampowered.com/app/2868840/Slay_the_Spire_2/)

## Homepage key art: “The Door Above”

### Story beat

Ari has reached the first impossible chamber. The Door is visible high across the ruin, cracked open by a thin cyan-to-ivory light. Between Ari and the threshold, three distant shapes have noticed the arrival.

The image should make the player think:

> I can see where I need to go. I cannot yet see everything between here and there.

### Camera

- Wide 16:9 frame.
- Very low camera, roughly knee height behind Ari.
- 20–24 mm equivalent lens.
- Seven-degree Dutch tilt, subtle rather than disorienting.
- Perspective rises from lower-left to upper-right.
- The Door is viewed in three-quarter perspective, never straight-on.

### Placement

- **Ari:** lower-left third, back to camera, about 8–10% of frame height.
- **Ascent:** broken steps, roots, and leaning masonry make an S-curve through the center.
- **Door:** upper-right third, roughly 30–35% of frame height, partially occluded by architecture.
- **Title space:** upper-left, within a dark, low-detail sky mass.
- **Menu space:** left-center, above and slightly inward from Ari.

### Threat ladder

Use three levels of enemy disclosure.

1. **Foreground implication:** a partial limb, eye, or indentation beneath a slab. It should be discovered on a second look.
2. **Mid-distance watcher:** a readable strange silhouette on a broken arch, separated by a thin cyan rim.
3. **Threshold guardian:** the smallest but clearest threat, standing directly against the warm light near the Door.

This creates the harrowing “climb through danger” read without turning the homepage into a combat scene.

### Light journey

The image should graduate bottom to top:

1. heavy indigo-black foreground;
2. moss/teal midground with intermittent cyan guidance;
3. a narrow cyan threshold seam;
4. pale amber and ivory immediately beyond the Door.

The warm light must not fill the whole sky. It should remain concentrated behind and around the Door so it reads as potential, not safety already granted.

Ari's scarf catches both cyan bounce light and a fine amber edge. That links the player visually to the destination.

### Door design

Keep the existing timber, dark metal bands, pale monolithic frame, and central seam. Evolve it through:

- a slight inward lean that feels physically impossible;
- asymmetrical damage and root intrusion;
- a top lintel that disappears into fog;
- carved subject glyphs that are barely visible until the seam pulses;
- one leaf opened only a few degrees, enough to spill a blade of light across the stairs.

The Door should feel ancient and intelligent, not merely locked.

## Rendering language

### Shape

- Strong asymmetry.
- Curves in living things, wedges and verticals in architecture.
- Enemies have one exaggerated shape each.
- Ari remains simple and readable, with a compact triangular silhouette created by coat, satchel, and scarf.

### Line

- Dark colored contours, not pure black everywhere.
- Thick outer silhouette, sparse interior line.
- Light may erase parts of a contour.
- Background objects lose line weight and contrast with distance.

### Value

Use five primary value bands:

1. foreground near-black;
2. dark environment;
3. midtone stone and fog;
4. cyan/saffron accents;
5. Door light.

If the image is readable in grayscale, the color will work.

### Texture

- Dry brush and chipped edges on stone.
- Long directional strokes in fog and sky to reinforce the climb.
- Wood grain only near the Door.
- No evenly distributed noise.
- No visible square pixels in the final key art.

### Character treatment

Ari should graduate from the placeholder sprite into a stylized illustrated figure:

- face mostly concealed from rear and three-quarter views;
- rounded natural hair silhouette;
- short teal coat with large, graphic folds;
- ochre/saffron scarf with an animated loose end;
- worn satchel carrying small paper and brass details;
- proportions slightly compressed, but not chibi;
- readable pose before costume detail.

## UI integration

The art and UI should occupy separate contrast zones.

- Place the Dungeon wordmark in the upper-left dark sky.
- Stack `Continue`, `Begin a Run`, `Archive`, and `Settings` at left-center.
- Use parchment-dark translucent shapes with irregular cut-paper edges, not glassmorphism.
- Let the focused menu item gain a saffron underline and a subtle cyan response in the Door.
- Keep resource counters and profile information small and peripheral.
- Do not put a large opaque panel over Ari, an enemy silhouette, or the light path.

The first selected action should be `Continue` when a save exists and `Enter the Dungeon` when it does not.

---

## Motion direction

Build an 8–12 second seamless ambient loop from the existing separated-plane workflow.

### Constant motion

- fog climbs slowly upward;
- dust reverses gravity near the threshold;
- Ari's scarf and coat hem respond to a faint pull from the Door;
- cyan light breathes at an uneven, almost biological cadence;
- a one-percent parallax drift creates depth.

### Rare motion

- the mid-distance watcher shifts its head once per loop;
- one pair of eyes appears in the lower dark, then is lost in fog;
- the Door opens a fraction on menu focus;
- a root tightens around stone during the brightest pulse.

### Interaction

- Moving selection sends a faint cyan current up one section of the path.
- Confirming a run pulls particles, scarf, and fog toward the Door before the cut.
- Reduced-motion mode retains light changes and removes camera/parallax movement.

## Production structure

Reuse the existing layered scene approach, but rebuild the composition into these functional groups:

1. `00_abyss_backplate`
2. `10_summit_light`
3. `20_far_ruins`
4. `30_door_and_frame`
5. `40_threshold_guardian`
6. `50_mid_ascent`
7. `60_mid_watcher`
8. `70_ari`
9. `80_foreground_threat`
10. `90_fog_and_particles`
11. `100_light_rays`
12. `110_color_grade`

Keep masks generous enough for parallax. Paint hidden overlap behind Ari, enemies, and the Door so no transparent gaps appear during motion.

---

## Guardrails

Avoid:

- a centered, symmetrical Door poster;
- a pure white portal with no color story;
- enemies posed like a combat lineup;
- high detail in every plane;
- generic skulls, gargoyles, or armored demons;
- copying Slay the Spire creature anatomy, UI frames, cards, or typography;
- making all darkness neutral gray;
- using orange across the whole sky;
- literal school supplies as monster bodies;
- cute mascot proportions;
- sacrificing title/menu legibility for spectacle.

The goal is not “Dungeon in the Slay the Spire 2 style.” The goal is:

> **Dungeon's world, made crisper, stranger, livelier, and more cinematic.**

## Concept-art prompt

```text
Use case: stylized-concept
Asset type: 16:9 game homepage key art, no UI and no text
Primary request: Reimagine Dungeon's existing monumental Door threshold as a cinematic bottom-to-top ascent. Preserve Ari, the saffron scarf, the cold cyan Door seam, pale ruined stone, dark timber, roots, rubble, and fog. Change the frontal symmetrical composition into a low, wide, three-quarter view that climbs from lower-left darkness toward an upper-right Door opened only a few degrees. Place three distant threats at different depths between Ari and the light: one barely implied in the foreground, one strange watcher silhouette on a broken arch, and one small guardian backlit at the threshold.
Input images: Image 1 is Dungeon's current threshold and identity reference; Image 2 is a composition and mood reference only.
Style/medium: original crisp graphic painterly 2D fantasy illustration; bold readable silhouettes; irregular hand-drawn contours; limited value groups; selective dry-brush texture; expressive but solemn; no visible pixel grid.
Composition/framing: wide 16:9, low camera behind Ari, subtle Dutch tilt, S-curve ascent, strong negative space in upper-left for a title and left-center for menu options.
Lighting/mood: indigo-black foreground to moss/teal midground to cyan seam and concentrated pale amber/ivory promise beyond the Door; enemies remain mostly in shadow with restrained rim light; harrowing but inviting.
Constraints: Ari is small but readable; Door remains the dominant destination; no combat pose; no text; no logo; no UI; no watermark.
Avoid: centered symmetry, generic gothic demons, cute mascots, uniform pixel art, photorealism, orange covering the whole sky, explicit imitation of another game's creatures or interface.
```
